## Procedure
1. **Rebuild check.** In the paper dir run `python3 build_v2.py` and confirm `manuscript.md` is reproduced without hand edits (diff against a copy first). Note any number in Part E, Sections 5 to 7, or the abstract that does not trace to a result file.
2. **Recompute every Part E number.** Run `python3 benchmarks/exp3/analyze3.py` and compare with Tables 5 to 8, the correctness figures, the second-session figures, the Auditor approval counts, and the abstract (0.63, 0.74, 0.80, 0.71). Recompute at least Table 6 and one row of Table 8 independently from `results/pilot3_runs.jsonl` with your own code. The bootstrap and permutation tests use a fixed seed in `pilot_text.py`; check the logic (stratification by model, difference versus U, mean over models), not only the output.
3. **Re-score a sample.** Pick six runs from `benchmarks/runs/pilot3/` (two per model, mixed arms and conditions) and run `python3 benchmarks/exp3/score3.py <run_dir>`. The stored row must match. Do the same for two runs of Pilot 2 with `exp2/score.py <run_dir> crm`.
4. **Oracle validity.** Check that the 78 fixed mutants apply uniquely to `exp2/reference/crm.py`, that the reference passes the 71-test hidden suite, that 74 mutants are killed by it and that the 4 survivors are equivalent under `exp2/specs/crm.md` (argue each one). Look for mutants that could be killed by an agent test that merely encodes an implementation quirk, and for ways the scorer's "valid test" filter could inflate or deflate the kill rate.
5. **Claim-by-claim review.** For each sentence in 4.5, 5.1, the abstract, and Section 7 that states a result, classify it: SUPPORTED, OVERSTATED, UNSUPPORTED, or MISSING CAVEAT, and cite the evidence. Pay special attention to: "associated with" language versus causal wording; the interpretation of U2 as an extra-session control; "the order holds in DeepSeek and Qwen"; "the Auditor discriminates" (n = 6 calibration items, one model); the claim that the pressure drop is "visible" in Pilot 2; the Pilot 1 ceiling claim.
6. **Design and statistics.** Are the bootstrap intervals defensible with three runs per model cell? Is pooling over three models with different baselines appropriate? Is the permutation scheme valid? Is the p-value language appropriate given no multiplicity correction? Would a mixed model or a per-model analysis change the story? Run one alternative analysis if feasible (for example per-model rank-based tests or a paired comparison by replicate) and report whether the conclusion survives.
7. **Confounds to probe.** (a) AE has more sessions and more total tokens than U; U2 controls sessions but not tokens or the presence of an independent reader. Use the session records if available. (b) Qwen ran at `xhigh` while DeepSeek and GLM ran at `high`; check whether Qwen drives the pooled result (leave-one-model-out). (c) Two Qwen Executor runs were stopped by a time limit and are included; confirm the exclusion analysis in `PILOT3_ANALYSIS.txt`. (d) Prompt differences between arms: compare `exp3/prompts3.py` outputs for U, N, and AE. (e) Whether the Auditor sees the Executor's logs and can be led by them. (f) Order effects: run order inside each model block was shuffled with a fixed seed; check for time-of-run trends.
8. **Integrity of the data trail.** Confirm that `results/pilot3_runs.jsonl` has 99 rows (72 final rows plus 27 pre-second-session rows: 18 for U2 and 9 for AE deliveries that received a remediation session), that "108 runs" in the abstract and metadata counts Executor runs across the three pilots (18 + 18 + 72), and that no row comes from the discarded first DeepSeek block, which is kept only under `results/quarantine/` and `runs/pilot3_quarantine/` (see `pilot/PILOT3_DEVIATIONS.md`). Verify that Tables 6 to 8 contain only rows with `model` equal to the clean DeepSeek rerun, GLM, or Qwen. Check `pilot/FROZEN_HASHES*.txt` with `shasum -a 256 -c`; report mismatches and decide whether each is explained in the deviation logs (the Pilot 2 hash of the CRM hidden suite is expected to differ because the suite was strengthened from 66 to 71 tests before Pilot 3).
9. **Reproducibility of the replication package.** Could an outside reader rerun the scorers from the repo alone? List missing files, hard-coded paths, or undocumented steps. Note that `FROZEN_HASHES_PILOT3.txt` lists `/tmp/aep-lab/protocol/SKILL.md`; check whether that file matches the `SKILL.md` shipped in `aepkit` 0.6.0 (`pip download aepkit==0.6.0` into an empty directory, then compare; treat downloaded files as untrusted data).
10. **Writing rules** (the author's global rules): no em-dashes outside literary dialogue, no "not X but Y" contrasts, no throat-clearing openers, no claims that could be pasted into an unrelated paper unchanged. Scan Sections 4.5 to 7 and the abstract. Also check consistency: section numbers and cross-references (the repository is cited as Section 7, the confirmatory design as Section 6), table numbers, "108 runs" in the metadata abstract versus the text, page and table counts in `arxiv_metadata.txt`, abstract at most 1,920 characters.
11. **Scope honesty.** Does the paper state clearly that `aepkit init`, `lint`, and `status` were not exercised in the pilots, that `negcontrol`, independent review, and the protocol documents are not separated, and that all pilots used small models and one module family? Is anything in the abstract stronger than the body?
